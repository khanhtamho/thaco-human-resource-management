# Human Resource Management Case Study: THACO

## Overview

This is an individual academic project completed as part of the Human Resource Management course.

The project analyzes human resource management challenges at Truong Hai Group Corporation (THACO), a Vietnamese multi-industry corporation. Using workforce and recruitment data from 2019 to 2021, I examine key HR issues, apply human resource management concepts, and propose practical recommendations to improve workforce planning, recruitment, employee development, performance management, and compensation and benefits.

## Project Objectives

- Identify key HR challenges based on workforce and recruitment data.
- Apply HR management theories to analyze the company's situation.
- Evaluate existing HR practices and identify potential areas for improvement.
- Propose solutions to support talent attraction, employee development, and retention.

## Key Areas of Analysis

### 1. Workforce Planning

Analyze workforce growth and recruitment demand to understand the challenges of aligning human resources with business expansion.

### 2. Recruitment and Selection

Examine recruitment demand, applicant conversion, recruitment costs, and employer branding to identify potential inefficiencies in the hiring process.

### 3. Learning and Development

Evaluate training activities and talent development programs, focusing on training completion and employee retention after development programs.

### 4. Performance Management

Explore how performance evaluation, feedback, and recognition can support employee motivation and development.

### 5. Compensation and Benefits

Assess salary competitiveness, reward policies, and employee benefits in relation to talent attraction, motivation, and retention.

## Key Insights

Based on the case analysis, the project identifies several HR challenges during the 2019–2021 period:

- **Recruitment pressure:** Recruitment demand increased substantially, while the proportion of applicants signing official employment contracts declined.
- **Recruitment costs and employer branding:** Heavy reliance on paid recruitment platforms highlighted opportunities to strengthen employer branding and diversify recruitment channels.
- **Talent retention:** Training investments did not always translate into strong employee retention, particularly among participants in talent development programs.
- **Compensation and motivation:** The analysis identified concerns about salary competitiveness and declining reward levels.
- **Integrated HR management:** Workforce planning, recruitment, training, performance management, and compensation need to work together to support long-term talent management.

These insights reflect the historical period and information covered in the academic case study, not THACO's current HR situation.

## Recommendations

The project proposes several areas for improvement:

- Strengthen workforce planning and monitor employee turnover.
- Improve employer branding, recruitment processes, and candidate experience.
- Diversify recruitment channels and build talent pipelines through university partnerships and internship programs.
- Connect training outcomes with job performance, career development, and succession planning.
- Establish clearer performance indicators and more consistent feedback practices.
- Review market salary benchmarks and align compensation with job responsibilities, employee capabilities, and performance.
